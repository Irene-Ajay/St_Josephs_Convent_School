Central Engineering Finding

Why There Are Two Systems: Deliberately Building Less for Long-Term Sustainability

We first designed a full enterprise architecture (PostgreSQL, Redis, Celery, Docker, MinIO, CI/CD, 8 modules, 157 files) and built ~40% of it. After visiting St. Joseph's Convent High School and speaking with staff, we abandoned it—not because the architecture was wrong, but because a school left with a system nobody can maintain is worse off than before the project arrived.

Engineering Question Full Architecture (`docs/` — Rejected) What We Built (`school-system/` — Delivered)
Who restarts it when it fails? Needs someone who understands Docker and Celery Close the window, run one command
What does it cost per month? ₹1,500–3,500, forever ₹0
How do you back it up? Managed cloud database snapshots Copy one file (`school.db`) to a pen drive
How many dependencies? 66 packages 9 packages
Who maintains it after we graduate? Unanswered A written non-technical manual (`SETUP.md`) and one file

Deliberately Excluded Features (With Stated Reasons)

  • 1. Automatic SMS (Excluded):

    Indian law (TRAI) mandates DLT registration by the school (~₹5,900 + GST, 3–7 working days, and legal entity documents). One-click WhatsApp achieves the same result today at ₹0 cost.

  • 2. Fee Management (Excluded):

    Handling money requires financial audit trails and reconciliation that the school did not ask for.

  • 3. Detailed Medical Records (Excluded):

    Sensitive health data about minors cannot be adequately protected in a school without a full-time system administrator. Only allergy and emergency contact information is collected.

  • 4. Cloud Hosting (Excluded):

    Creates a permanent monthly bill and a cloud billing account someone must own after the student team graduates.
